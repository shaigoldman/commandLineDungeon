# A Register of Things Still Owed

*Author's lodestone, volume II. Read after The Unfinished Noon. These entries supply continuity, motives, and material details for writers; they are not an encyclopedia available to the adventurer. A vignette illustrates character or custom and need not be a recorded event witnessed by every inhabitant. Established history follows volume I. Matters marked uncertain remain open.*

## Country, institutions, and passages

### Ashfall

The name belongs first to the volcanic watershed, then to its settlements and overlapping political claims. Rich soil and dangerous dust shaped its broad eaves, white doorposts, and communal waterworks. The outside country survived the Unfinished Noon. Its precise present condition is not known to most trapped inhabitants, and the dungeon must never casually stand for the whole world.

A child once asked Hob whether ash was dirty snow. He answered by planting barley in one palmful and watching the other melt. He remembers the question whenever the frost vaults whiten his sleeves.

### The Covenant of the Open Bowl

An agreement older than imperial sovereignty: water, transport, and passage are owed to a stranger in immediate danger, with payment considered afterward. Its thumb-marked clay witnesses made consent visible without requiring literacy. Its descendants dispute how far its obligations extend, especially when helping one stranger endangers a shelter.

A keeper offered a wounded tax collector water from an unmarked bowl. When he asked whose property he had drunk, she named the spring, the dead builders, and tomorrow's thirsty people. He wrote down only the spring. She made him another cup.

### Pallu and the Deep Bowl assemblies

Pallu belonging grows through shared maintenance, adoption, and responsibility for wells and terraces. The Deep Bowl assemblies organized resistance to compulsory extraction. They disagree over negotiation, sabotage, and duties to people dependent on imperial heat. Their objection is to coerced obligation, not to every invention or foreign visitor.

During a bitter assembly, an apprentice repaired a leaking joint beneath the speakers. Nobody noticed until their feet dried. The minutes record both speeches in full and the apprentice merely as “present.” Moss-Ear later had her name entered above his own.

### Awa and the Returning Road

Awa carriers maintain relationships as carefully as routes. Names sewn inside coat hems remember absent travelers; the Returning Road alliance uses those relationships to search for missing people and move forbidden shadow remnants. Wealthy Awa trading houses sometimes oppose it. Shared customs create arguments as readily as solidarity.

An imperial inspector confiscated a coat for its suspicious stitching. Its owner asked him to arrest the sleeves separately: each represented relatives in another jurisdiction. The joke delayed him long enough for a medicine parcel to leave by the back stair. The coat was never returned.

### Vraskii and the Watch of the High Passes

Vraskii communities keep crossings open through winter and hang bells where travelers might otherwise disappear unheard. The Watch favors vigilance against catastrophic dependence. Other families emphasize their children employed below the observatory and the warmth sustaining vulnerable villages. Neither position makes the other imaginary.

A watcher rang the pass bell through a storm after a caravan was already overdue. Asked whether she expected an answer, she said the travelers might need a direction even if she could not hear them. Her successor still repairs the rope before sharpening his knife.

### The royal observatory

Originally a public house for weather measurements, it became a palace of extraction under Vey. Beneath later triumphal stonework survives its first lintel: leave room for the weather. Kitchens, gardens, workshops, and municipal housing made it a lived institution rather than an isolated wizard's tower.

Mabel delivered bread through its public entrance until guards redirected suppliers underground. She complained that the new route bruised her loaves. Years later she admitted that she had kept complaining because somebody ought to remember when the front door had belonged to everyone.

### Forgotten stone and the old well approaches

The recurring stone halls preserve civic records, meeting spaces, and approaches to wells. Numbers removed from vessels, thumb-shaped hollows in clay, and doorsteps polished by queues make useful traces. Reappearance does not mean a new identical kingdom has been discovered; the fold reconnects old obligations unevenly.

Ada found her mother's ration number scratched beside an official casualty total. She rubbed the total clean enough to read and left the smaller number dusty. She could bear one task that morning, and chose the one a stranger might need.

### Mossbound ruins

These remnants of medicinal gardens and living filters were infrastructure as well as cultivated beauty. Roots cleaned circulating water, herbs supplied apothecaries, and workers ate in the shade. Concentrated extraction later altered their ecology. Not every bright fungus or restless vine requires a secret intelligence.

Fenwick labels seedlings with the names of people who supplied them. When a fashionable scholar called this imprecise classification, he showed her three identical nettles that flourished under different conditions. “They came from different kitchens,” he said. She borrowed a cutting and asked whose kitchen she should write down.

### Frost vaults

Well-silvered mirrors and vulnerable materials were kept here under controlled cold. Their ice is engineered storage meeting the fold's uneven intervals, not proof that Ashfall's surface lies under eternal winter. Footprints may end where an opening moved. Careful writers distinguish an absent traveler from a confirmed death.

Orla warmed her husband's work gloves before their last delivery together. Now she warms an empty pair when she must enter a vault. She knows exactly how foolish it looks and would prefer that nobody demonstrate kindness by mentioning it.

### Ember depths

Furnaces, release chambers, and workshops supplied the engine's Fire functions. Soot, tool marks, evacuation rails, and maintenance notices belong beside dangerous machinery. The place once employed tired people who wanted their shifts to end. Its heat should suggest labor and accumulated pressure before suggesting a generic underworld.

Hob remembers the furnace gong announcing supper. A regulator now makes nearly the same sound. He once reached for his bowl before remembering where he stood, then was angry with the machine for the rest of the day.

### The shelter court

Niblet's court is a compact among storeroom keepers and survivors. Its authority rests on shelter, supplies, and labor agreements rather than hereditary dominion. The court's inventory comes through salvage bargains; generosity without replacement would exhaust it. That practical truth does not absolve its unfair demands.

A visitor bowed to the saucepan crown. Niblet bowed back so quickly that the handle struck a shelf. Ada entered the damaged crockery under royal expenses. He asked whether dignity could be charged instead. She said that account had already exceeded its limit.

## People and the offices they carry

### Emperor Vey of the Thirteenth Bell

Vey remembers names, listens attentively, and treats consent as something his eventual success can justify afterward. He knowingly ordered the conditional second sun made present without preserving its conditions. His surviving bargains offer genuine power through the same habit of transferring costs to other lives. His courtesy remains intact inside his sealed suite.

He once interrupted a treasury meeting to ask after a clerk's sick daughter. He remembered her name correctly. Before the meeting ended, he approved a levy that took away the household's heating allowance. He would have considered it unkind to confuse the two decisions.

### Ruthagor, the Listener Under Stone

An ancient dragon whose body holds continuities, Ruthagor first preserved the watershed's springs and later braced the regulating wheel against erasure. His immediate guardianship has become possessive. Defeating him releases a circuit; it does not erase his earlier rescue or repair every wound. His resistance to Annihilate arises from the density of duration he holds.

A delegation once demanded proof that he remembered their drowned village. He recited the names of its dogs before its magistrates. The survivors laughed, then cried, then argued about whether one dog had actually belonged to the miller. Ruthagor listened to the correction.

### Ergathon, Ruthagor's chosen

Younger and impatient, Ergathon admires instruments, locks, and the appearance of settled authority. He guards an earlier approach, copying a severity he mistakes for his teacher's wisdom. His devotion mixes affection, ambition, and fear of being dismissed. His name is a personal identity; encounter presentation may clothe that role in different generated bodies.

He collected a lock with no surviving key and asked Ruthagor whether guarding it constituted faithful service. Ruthagor said that first he should discover what it kept closed. Ergathon polished it instead, which was easier and looked more like progress.

### Awathon

The Awa swordsmith predates Vey by centuries. Official poetry made him an unconquered warrior; household evidence preserves a gardener with an injured wrist who paid bereaved families. He reclaimed his broadsword when its captain broke a promise to escort grain. Leaving the captain alive is part of his legacy.

A young admirer asked how many enemies the sword had defeated. Awathon named the villages reached by the grain wagons. When pressed, he offered the boy a hoe and said there was work for somebody determined to count strokes. Later portraits omitted the hoe.

### Chief Moss-Ear

Associated with Pallu negotiation, Moss-Ear wears a mantle recalling a spring preserved during drought. He distrusts eloquence because a good speech can make deprivation sound shared while leaving its distribution unchanged. His compromises keep some people alive and cost him the trust of others.

At a feast he placed a cracked bowl beside the chief speaker's silver cup. When challenged, he said both held water perfectly while empty. He then listened to the speech, refilled the cracked bowl twice, and refused to explain which part of the evening had been the demonstration.

### Chief Twelve Hats

The nickname remembers headpieces carried on behalf of absent Awa households. It is representative duty made portable, not mere eccentric costume. Twelve Hats enjoys wit but becomes cold when amusement replaces attention to those households. An encountered chief's generated affiliation need not turn a shared nickname into an ethnic rule.

A child asked whether the hats made him taller. He crouched until their eyes were level and said they mostly made doorways lower. Then he let her choose which household's story she wanted to hear. He carried that hat in his hands afterward.

### Chiefs Cinderwhisk and Puddlethrone

These names anchor opposing Vraskii arguments. Cinderwhisk would destroy the wheel before dependence deepens; Puddlethrone fears the deaths caused by sudden shutdown. The latter's reservoir childhood gives his supposedly embarrassing title a political history: thaw water, managed patiently, sustains people whom dramatic decisions overlook.

Cinderwhisk once called him a ruler of puddles. Puddlethrone asked how many children had drowned in a metaphor. Neither apologized. During the next flood they worked on opposite ends of the same sluice, shouting practical instructions above the water. Their disagreement survived because they did.

### King Niblet the Underprepared

His saucepan crown dates from a collapsing storehouse, when he held a door after actual officers fled. The title honors that act but does not guarantee his later judgment. He can listen, hide behind procedure, or do both in the same conversation. “Underprepared” remains a reminder that courage preceded any claim to competence.

He keeps the saucepan's dent facing outward. A smith offered to straighten it and received a surprisingly sharp refusal. Later Niblet brought the smith a better meal than his own, without an explanation. The smith began repairing the court's damaged hinges first.

### Mabel Crumb

A baker for the observatory and now a supplier to scattered shelters, Mabel remembers institutions through orders, flour shortages, and who ate standing up. She undercounts portions when hunger has become embarrassing. Her festival cake shaped like a second sun was an imperial commission, not evidence she understood the experiment.

An astronomer once asked for a cake without a shadow. Mabel proposed eating it in darkness, then charged for two extra lamps when he failed to laugh. She later saved his unpaid order slip because its date was one of the last things everyone still agreed upon.

### Pip Bellwether

Keeper of twelve public bells, Pip understood their social purpose before he heard the extra resonance between the twelfth stroke and silence. He fears a sound awaiting permission, not a hidden physical thirteenth tower bell. His care for timing includes the workers whose days depended on shared hours.

A foreman wanted an earlier morning stroke without moving the evening one. Pip asked whether the missing interval should come from the emperor's sleep. The foreman reported him for insolence. Pip retained the report: it confirmed that someone had once believed an hour could be measured honestly.

### Orla Soot

Orla's household silvered mirrors; her missing husband delivered the final lower-chamber instrument. The haircut story lets her approach his terror sideways. His fate remains unresolved. Authors must not turn a passing clue into proof of death, a captive location, or an implemented rescue objective.

She trims one side of her own hair shorter now, claiming it saves time. Once, while comparing two flawed mirrors, she smiled at an uneven reflection before she could stop herself. She polished the glass so thoroughly afterward that her fingers bled through the cloth.

### Bram Pickle

A neighborhood assessment clerk, Bram recorded people who fainted under the shadow levy rather than recording only what the palace collected. He is patient with frightened customers and exacting with officials who ask for convenient approximations. His surname comes from a household trade; preservation matters more to him than prestige.

When ordered to summarize several missing residents as “miscellaneous,” he asked how many miscellaneous persons should receive a widow's allowance. The supervisor sent the ledger back. Bram ruled a wider column and wrote each name again, smaller but still legible.

### Tansy Bolt

A technician from a family of observatory maintainers, Tansy obeyed a signed bypass order and pocketed the regulating screw it displaced. Her responsibility is real, though neither total nor equivalent to Vey's informed command. She mistrusts phrases that make a decision sound like a property of machinery.

She keeps tools sorted by the injury each can prevent. Visitors assume the labels are jokes: burnt thumb, broken wrist, widow. She lends the first two readily. Asked about the third, she says that one requires its instructions read aloud before she will release it.

### Hob Nettle

Fuel hauler, herb grower, and an attentive witness to heat, smells, and meals, Hob knows the furnace neighborhood through his body. His account that the sun sneezed is an honest sensory description. He has no privileged knowledge that the actual sun was damaged or replaced.

He grows nettles in an imperial inspection tray. Its raised letters promise efficient use of public resources. Hob says it is the only official promise he can personally guarantee, then pulls on patched gloves and harvests enough for a neighbor's soup.

### Dora Teaspoon

Dora marks lintels to track a kitchen displaced from its original street. Her irritation about a kettle protects a larger grief: hospitality needs a place where people can expect to find you. The familiar claim that her kitchen is on floor nine is her reckoning, not a guaranteed map coordinate.

She once scolded a stranger for muddy boots before discovering that the boots were all he owned. She made him sit while she washed them. When he thanked her, she said she was protecting the floor. Neither of them believed her, and both let the explanation stand.

### Fenwick Moss

An observatory gardener's descendant, Fenwick preserves seeds and helps remove unwanted ownership marks from weapons. He knows that Ruthagor destroyed a dangerous operating text containing useful shutdown knowledge. His story that the dragon ate the manual makes a terrible unilateral choice small enough to repeat in public.

He buried an arsenal plaque beneath a bed of beans. When Dora called that disrespectful to history, he showed her the names copied on paper and the beans already climbing. He had no quarrel with remembering. He objected to giving a plaque the better soil.

### Ada Candle

Ada preserved ration records inside candle molds and now scrutinizes Niblet's labor accounts. She distinguishes declared holidays from actual rest. Her humor measures the distance between official language and lived cost; she is not simply a universal cynic. A promise fulfilled earns her practical loyalty.

Niblet once announced that every worker deserved a day free of worry. Ada brought him the shift roster and asked which day he would cover. He took a broom. She corrected his grip, said nothing complimentary, and remained beside him until the corridor was clean.

### Nell Thread

Nell prepares the dead and keeps duplicate names in embroidery. She understands haunting through unfinished relations, not through a doctrine that every ghost wants revenge. Her antidote knowledge belongs to an undertaking craft that recognizes harmful bindings as well as bodily poison.

A customer apologized for bringing a torn shroud. Nell asked whether its owner had always dressed perfectly. On hearing that he had not, she left one neat visible patch rather than hiding it. “He should be recognizable,” she said, and gave the unused cloth back.

### Bix Turnip

Bix found Tansy's displaced screw inside a root grown near the maintenance drain. His concern for the vegetable is partly practical: seeds and food remain valuable below ground. His speculative question about growing a moon is a gardener's way of handling evidence he cannot yet place.

He washed the screw, then apologized to the turnip for making it look less remarkable. He replanted its top in a bowl. Someone laughed, so he asked whether they had a better method for obtaining leaves. They brought him another bowl the next morning.

### Wren Lastlight

A junior recorder of mirror differences, Wren removed a resonant tuning handbell when every reflection showed an empty room. She carries technical fragments toward a possible repair without possessing complete cosmological knowledge. Her three-part plan remains background intent, not a quest the current game fulfills.

She practiced turning the handbell over without letting its clapper touch. Pip watched once and asked what hour she hoped to announce. “None,” she said. He nodded as if she had finally described a difficult piece of music accurately, then left before she could ask him to help.

## Creatures and unfinished relations

### Dragons

Dragons accumulate duration in their bodies and hoard continuities more deeply than valuables. Their ancient bargains can preserve villages while overlooking individual lifetimes. They disagree about their own origins. Whether they arose from remembering places or merely learned to inhabit them remains uncertain.

A dragon once refused a chest of gold but accepted a broken cradle repaired through six generations. The donor thought this proved dragons sentimental. Ruthagor called that an inadequate description of a thing which had continued to hold children after everyone who made it was gone.

### Shadows

Hostile Shadows condense from possibilities severed by extraction. They seek the warmth of living relationships, including villagers and chiefs, without representing an owner's secret evil. Their darkness is deprivation. Recognizable habits may appear in them without permitting confident identification of a particular original person.

Bram saw one pause beside an assessment post and extend its arm. He put down his ledger. The thing attacked the lantern instead of him, and he never claimed this was mercy. He copied the posture later because he feared somebody would ask him to forget it.

### Robots and golems

Robots obey recorded maintenance instructions; golems interpret jointly witnessed obligations. Their different kinds of fidelity explain both their usefulness and their danger. A robot's failed sealed housing may detonate. A golem may defend an obligation whose beneficiaries no longer live where the maker expected.

A golem stood over an empty cistern while people carried water past it. A robot beside it repeatedly requested a supervisor's authorization to leave. The survivors painted a bucket on the robot's chest so newcomers would stop mistaking the request for an offer of assistance.

### Gremlins and Dark Fairies

Gremlins gather possibilities concentrated as gold into individual pots. Dark Fairies accumulate fortune through unacknowledged bargains or theft; “dark” names a relation, not hereditary moral corruption. Their rich remnants arise from stored fortune. Neither creature's entire culture should be inferred from a hostile encounter.

A gremlin rejected a shining coin and pursued the dirty one rolling beneath a cabinet. Pip guessed it preferred older money. Mabel said it preferred the coin somebody was presently desperate to recover. They watched until the creature vanished and agreed only that it had worked very hard.

### Wild Villagers, zombies, and ghosts

Wild Villagers remain living people under sickness, isolation, or fear. Zombies repeat instructions after personal choice has largely departed. Ghosts hold a person through a violently unfinished relation. Water's Befriend and the Cleanser can create viable social coherence without proving every creature was originally human; authors should retain that ambiguity.

Nell once heard a zombie knock before entering a ruined pantry. She stepped aside and cried afterward. Dora asked what it had wanted. Nell said she did not know, and Dora put away the spare cup she had already begun to fetch.

### Spiders, snakes, and parasites

Spiders favor warm hunting places; snakes seek damp masonry and shelter; parasites descend from the living filters and exploit vitality loosened by extraction. Medicine, pressure, and displacement intensify their danger. Their ecology supplies enough meaning without assigning every animal a hidden royal identity.

Hob discovered a snake living behind his warmest seed tray. He moved the tray, then found the snake following. “A paying tenant would be welcome,” he told it. He finally moved the nettles instead. The arrangement lasted until the corridor changed its connection.

## Instruments, stores, and surviving objects

### The moon engine, mirror, and thirteenth resonance

The silver regulating wheel gave the engine its name; it never controlled the moon. Fire releases possibilities, Water maintains identity, and Wind opens routes. A well-silvered mirror showed a conditional second sun. Vey demanded the result without its sustaining choices. The extra resonance occupies the interval between the twelfth stroke and silence; Wren's handbell tunes that system rather than containing a miniature apocalypse.

Tansy once chalked three arrows beside a regulator. An official replaced them with one imperial arrow pointing forward. She drew the other two back after he left.

### Gold, crowns, jewels, and charms

Imperial coins retain usable stamped weight. Jewels and crowns release measured faculties when stabilized with gold; their named attribute and inspected cost govern their actual effect. Charms lend a faculty while carried rather than permanently. Doublers use stable surviving reflections to multiply their indicated faculty. None bestows hereditary authority.

Niblet tried on a Health Crown and asked whether it improved his claim. Ada said it might improve his chances of surviving the claim. He paid the stabilization cost himself, then insisted the transaction should remain outside the royal accounts.

### Medicines and the kitchen tradition

Health potions concentrate bodily repair in different doses; reusable vessels retain a finite charge. Mana potions and Super Potions replenish different resources as their descriptions specify. Poison Antidote breaks poison and curses; Poison Antibody grants lasting poison immunity without protecting against curses. Mystery and Poison Potions preserve unstable preparations with genuine risks, not secret guaranteed rewards.

Mabel labels a particularly bright batch “soup” because the frightened will sometimes accept food before medicine. Nell adds the dosage underneath. Neither believes the joke excuses an unreadable label.

### Candy, pills, and lessons

Candy began as portable stored practice for workers. Ordinary, Rare, and Ultra Candy confer differing experience; the EXP Pill preserves a concentrated lesson. Hard Candy doubles the next five experience gains. Bad Candy strips experience embodied as enemy levels. Cursed Candy supplies advancement with an unpaid binding attached.

A trainee asked whether swallowing enough lessons would let him skip apprenticeship. The instructor handed him a sweet and a damaged hinge. By sunset he could name every part beautifully and still needed somebody to hold the door. He returned next morning without asking again.

### Curses and costly exchanges

Cursed Gold and Cursed Potion offer real benefit alongside curse. The binding is an obligation attached to extraction, not evidence that its victim deserves suffering. Greedy Amulets turn bodily vitality into money; Health Pot spends gold on health; Health Drain and Mana Rock trade health for mana, while Mana Shard performs a larger finite exchange. Spiked Potion sacrifices present health for a chosen lasting faculty.

Bram writes the price before the benefit when copying these devices' labels. Sellers complain it discourages business. He replies that their customers are still buying, which is the part he finds troubling.

### Keys and permissions

Standard Keys satisfy ordinary chest custody. Rare Keys reach secret doors as well. Stair Keys belong to a distinct administrative system and are never substitutes for either. Reusable keys retain witnessed access for a limited number of openings. Wind's Unseal bypasses chest and door requirements without claiming a general right to every threshold.

Dora showed a locksmith five keys to her vanished pantry. He asked which was correct. She said that depended on which side of the pantry she approached. He charged for examining one lock and declined to specify how many sides he believed it possessed.

### Weapons, armor, and Awathon's broadsword

Fragile, Iron, Steel, Lucky, Magic, and Obsidian equipment reflects different material traditions and compromises in weight, fortune, power, and service life. Shields occupy a hand and sacrifice offense for protection; armor provides its inspected lasting protection. Awathon's broad blade retains the gardener's notch beneath its grip and the burden of a promise broken by its first captain.

Fenwick once refused to polish that notch away. The owner complained it made the weapon look used. Fenwick said this was among its more defensible qualities, then returned it sharper than before.

### The economical workshop

Gentle Grips preserve durability through learned economical touch. Sharpening Stones enhance a carried weapon's contribution; Skill Books relieve weight penalties through practiced movement. Anvils upgrade an unequipped bag weapon, Blade Shards repair one, and Magic Scabbards double a selected charged item's remaining service. These are distinct functions, not interchangeable universal restoration.

Tansy caught a merchant selling one scabbard under three descriptions. She made him demonstrate all three. He completed the first, explained the second at length, and offered her lunch before reaching the third. She accepted lunch and corrected his sign.

### Staves, Magic Books, and Mana Stars

Staves channel Wind, Fire, Water, or stranger resonances through basic magical attacks that consume mana. Magic Books choose a spell unpredictably; Mana Stars subsidize one available casting, Double Mana Stars two. Mana Gems and Diamonds reduce spell costs while carried. Magic Scales coordinate strength and magic through whichever faculty is greater.

An apprentice carved a star into his ordinary walking stick and demanded a free spell. His tutor asked which account would pay for it. He painted over the star but kept the stick; it remained excellent for walking.

### Alchemy and failed forms

Alchemy Books transform one selected item, Wands a whole carried collection, with gold costs and a possibility of failure. Mana Channel exchanges present mana for gold. Gold Chalice exchanges gold for a rare item; Magic Wand produces an elemental staff from a chosen object. Trash is sometimes a failed transformation and sometimes simply rubbish. Its unimportance can be true.

Bix traded a bent spoon to an alchemist and received a straight spoon of spectacular rarity. Dora examined it, stirred her soup, and said she would reserve judgment until it survived washing.

### Bags, hands, and traveling sight

Bigger and Huge Bags enlarge ordinary capacity; Infinite Bags maintain a folded opening without a carried-item limit. Extra Hand adds an equipment slot, born from workshop assistive craft rather than a required bodily transformation. Binoculars extend sight. Night Vision Goggles reveal the present floor through accelerated optical invention, not supernatural knowledge of every future floor.

Awa carriers tie a bright thread around an Infinite Bag's mouth. One explained that infinity was manageable; setting it down somewhere and forgetting which plain brown bag it was proved the greater difficulty.

### Routes, concealment, and summons

Escape Ropes guarantee combat escape; Teleporters offer reusable relocation. Stairfinders locate stairs once, Ultrastairfinders repeatedly, without satisfying their locks or removing guardians. Stealth Shoes suppress ambushes for their measured duration; carried Invisibility Cloaks suppress them continuously. Summoner Stones expend finite charges calling nearby creatures, while Wind's Summon more often brings people. None chooses a named missing relative.

Orla asked a seller whether a stone could find her husband. For once he abandoned his patter immediately. He showed her the honest label. She bought thread instead and thanked him by name.

### Communal energy and magnets

Energy pods descend from replenishment stations. Harnesses divert a selected pod into an item; Energy Generators create limited batches; Energy Magnets draw an existing pod for use. Mana Pods replenish mana after basic attacks. Item Magnets gather floor objects into nearby open spaces rather than conjuring ownership. Their convenience carries traces of infrastructure once maintained for many people.

Hob calls every surviving station “the public kitchen.” When a collector scraped that phrase off a casing, Hob wrote it back in soot. It lasted until somebody touched it, which happened often.

### Missiles, shocks, and forbidden releases

Throwing knives, javelins, bombs, boomerangs, handcuffs, and ballistic missiles belong to different arsenals and trades compressed by borrowed invention. Their inspected effects differ; handcuffs weaken rather than merely wound. Giant Bombs harm their thrower and nearby actors. Seismic Shockers distribute fixed armor-ignoring harm indiscriminately. Nukes destroy monsters and an unshielded user; Nuke Shields protect against one such release. These devices are genuine dangers, not comic anachronisms.

An arsenal clerk wrote “point away from curtains” beneath an official warning. His daughter recognized the handwriting years later. She remembered that he had been meticulous about curtains, and became afraid of everything else the warning had omitted.

### Measured advantages

Permanent stat preparations preserve a measured improvement; temporary boosters borrow intensity for a limited calculation span. The distinction grew from training versus emergency shift work. Critical Stars arrange one landed critical strike. Speedy Boots offer one near-certain evasion of an enemy attack, whereas Stealth Shoes prevent ambushes during travel. Their similar names should never blur their different effects.

An apprentice put both pairs of shoes on the same requisition. The quartermaster asked whether he intended to escape notice or escape a blow. “Ideally both,” he said. She approved one pair and wrote that the empire remained committed to economical optimism.
